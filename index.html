STEAM Studio

STEAM Studio labs for flexible K–12 implementation.

STEAM Studio is one of the studio’s main program offerings. These fourteen labs are designed as flexible 4–6 week experiences at roughly 30 hours total and can run as workshops, camps, a la carte school experiences, quarter bundles, or semester pathways.

How the lab model works

Each lab includes guided instruction, project cards, videos or demonstrations, applied builds, portfolio artifacts, and age-appropriate assessments or reflections.

Format: 4–6 weeks or condensed workshop/camp
Time: ~30 hours total per lab
Use cases: school enrichment, homeschool pods, summer camps, self-paced curriculum, semester pathways
Pricing: most labs range from $150–$400 as a 4–6 week lab depending on complexity and equipment

Programs & pathways

Programs are organized as studio pathways, not just standalone bundles.

Technology & Systems Design is the full pathway framework, with specialized pathways branching in the upper grades. Applied Digital Foundations is the parallel scaffolded pathway for neurodiverse and visual-first learners.

STEAM Exploration Studio

For ages 6–9. A guided elementary pathway built around exploration, building, creative technology, and introductory interactive design.

Labs: STEAM Exploration, Blocks Engineering, Immersive Systems, Digital Design
Capstone: interactive build showcase
Use cases: elementary enrichment, camps, homeschool pods, lower school studio blocks
Semester pricing: $1,800–$2,800 hybrid per classroom

Technology & Systems Design Foundations

For ages 9–12. A middle years feeder program centered on digital literacy, coding logic, simulations, systems thinking, and interactive design.

Labs: Programming Foundations, Game Design, Immersive Systems, Systems & Smart Tech
Capstone: interactive simulation or smart system project
Program focus: digital literacy, coding logic, collaboration, systems thinking
Semester pricing: $1,800–$3,200 hybrid per classroom

Technology & Systems Design (TSD)

For ages 13–17. This is the studio’s full high school pathway framework. Students begin with broad technology foundations, then move into specialized pathways such as Programming & Web, Digital Design, Game/XR, and Engineering.

Foundation sequence: Digital Information Technology → Discovering Computer Science or Game & Simulation Foundations
Specialized pathways: Programming & Web, Digital Design, Game/XR, Engineering
Capstone: portfolio, simulation, web product, engineering prototype, or advanced interactive system
Semester pricing: $2,500–$4,000 hybrid per classroom

TSD should be presented as the umbrella pathway, with the specialized tracks shown underneath rather than as totally separate unrelated programs.

Applied Digital Foundations (ADF)

A scaffolded pathway for learners who benefit from lower-text, visual-first, project-based, and neurodiverse-friendly instruction.

Progression: Applied Digital Foundations I → Applied Digital Exploration → Applied Interactive Systems → Applied Digital Applications
Core labs: Adapted STEAM Exploration, Immersive Systems, Digital Design, Intro Programming, Media & Applied Projects
Capstone: guided interactive portfolio project
Semester pricing: $2,500–$4,000 hybrid per classroom

TSD Specialized Pathways

Upper-pathway specializations can be marketed as pathway clusters within the larger TSD framework.

Programming & Web: Programming Foundations, Web Development, App Development
Digital Design: Digital Design, Content Creation, Research & Digital Publishing
Game / XR: Game Design, Immersive Systems, Systems & Smart Tech
Engineering: Robotics, 3D Design, Systems & Smart Tech, Blocks-to-Maker progression

Media & Entrepreneurship Studio

An optional studio pathway for creator-focused learners who want brand, media, publishing, and digital storytelling experiences.

LFDA Overview

LFDA blends academic learning with creative technology in a studio-inspired format. Learners experience CoreWorks academic support alongside TechQuest STEAM enrichment while growing digital literacy, project skills, and independent thinking.

The program is built for flexible placement, differentiated instruction, and partnership with families seeking a thoughtful blend of academics and technology learning.

CoreWorks

Academic learning, individualized support, differentiated instruction, literature, mathematics, science, social studies, Spanish, and structured academic progress.

CoreWorks supports learners with scaffolded lesson sequences, small-group work, and intentional academic routines.

TechQuest

STEAM enrichment through coding, robotics, engineering, maker design, immersive technology, game design, digital media, and portfolio projects.

TechQuest is built around studio-style learning, creative technology labs, and project-focused exploration that connects to the academic day.

Seasonal Program

Get ready for the new school year through creative, hands-on learning.

The LFDA Back-to-School Intensive gives learners an opportunity to explore creative technology, digital design, STEAM projects, collaborative activities, and structured studio experiences before the regular school year begins.

A Day at LFDA

A balanced day of technology, academics, movement, and meaningful work.

LFDA learners begin the day in TechQuest Lab, where they explore coding, engineering, digital design, immersive technology, and project-based learning. After the midday transition, learners continue into CoreWorks for differentiated academics, literature, science, social studies, Spanish, and applied learning.

9:00 AM–12:00 PM TechQuest Lab

Studio-based project learning in technology, design, and creative production.

12:00 PM–12:30 PM Lunch and Midday Transition

Learners pause for lunch, reset materials, socialize, and prepare for the afternoon academic block.

12:30 PM–3:00 PM CoreWorks Lab

Focused academic learning, inquiry, and applied work in language, math, science, social studies, and Spanish.

View TechQuest Rhythm

9:00–9:15 AM Class Meeting and Announcements — Learners organize materials, review priorities, and set goals.

9:15–9:45 AM Interactive Lesson — A short presentation, tutorial, guided activity, or discussion.

9:45–10:45 AM Guided Project Work — Learners apply the lesson through coding, design, building, or research.

10:45–11:00 AM Snack and Movement Break

11:00–11:45 AM Independent or Collaborative Project Work — Continued development, testing, revision, and progress sharing.

11:45–12:00 PM Reflection, Project Sharing, and Clean-Up — Learners save work, organize equipment, and set the next goal.

TechQuest projects generally develop across four-to-six-week lab units, allowing learners to move from guided instruction into planning, development, testing, revision, presentation, and portfolio documentation.

View CoreWorks Rhythm

12:45–1:10 PM Whole-Group Mini-Lesson — Presentation, demonstration, or shared academic discussion.

1:10–1:50 PM Differentiated Work Cycle — Independent, partner, or small-group academic work.

1:50–2:00 PM Movement Break

2:00–2:30 PM Applied Learning Block — Science investigations, writing, research, or project-based work.

2:30–3:00 PM Spanish or Closing Work Block — Spanish on Tuesday/Thursday; reflection and portfolio work Monday/Wednesday.

Learner differentiation

Lessons and project expectations are differentiated by learner readiness. Michelangelo learners receive additional visual directions, guided examples, planning templates, and step-by-step support, while Da Vinci learners receive extension challenges, deeper analysis, independent research, and opportunities to complete more advanced work.
